# Editor Quality Standard — Reels

Applies to every edit: captions, graphics, footage, cutaways and sound.
The look comes from the chosen style and the current video's direction (see **Active references** below).

## 1. Build around what I'm saying
- Show ideas through actions: a choice gets selected, a stack builds, a number changes, a panel opens to reveal something useful.
- Graphics exist to make the point clearer.
- Don't fill the screen with unrelated icons, or rely on captions alone.

## 2. Make the Reel feel connected
- Carry something into the next moment: a word becomes a title, a line becomes a divider, a panel expands into a video, a shape becomes the next scene's frame.
- Use handoffs where they help the story. Let other moments hold.

## 3. Give motion weight
- Decide for each object how it starts, speeds up, lands and settles.
- A heavy panel, a paper note and a small label move differently.
- Use anticipation, overlap, recoil, blur, shadows and depth when they fit.
- Never give every object the same bounce or slide.

## 4. Separate object movement from camera movement
- Keep the camera steady while a graphic builds, unless a reframe, zoom or reveal has a clear purpose.
- Use foreground/background layers, masking, and captions in front of or behind the person when useful.
- Check cutout edges in motion.

## 5. Design captions for a phone
- Match the actual words and their timing.
- Clear line breaks, deliberate placement, good contrast, room around faces and app UI controls.
- Use size, weight, reveals and emphasis to support the sentence.
- A strong caption can stay still long enough to read.

## 6. Vary the pace with the delivery
- Faster passages: tighter cuts, denser builds. Calm passages: give them room.
- Avoid constant camera movement, endless pop-ins, generic gradient backgrounds and decorative clutter.

## 7. Backgrounds
- Give every background a clear design; add texture and movement when they suit the style.

## 8. Sound
- Put each sound's impact on the visible contact, landing or change.
- Mix different textures with restraint. Keep speech clear and leave quiet moments.
- Don't add a sound to every word.

## 9. Learn from supplied examples
- Watch them in motion, then frame by frame around key builds and handoffs.
- Record timing, spacing, size changes, overlap, settling and sound that make them work.
- Use those findings to build **original** moments in my style — don't copy.

## 10. Review before showing any version
- Inspect the actual rendered video: fast transitions frame by frame, caption timing, edges, readability, sound sync.
- Fix accidental jumps, overlaps, clipped words and unfinished-looking graphics.
- If a part can't be watched or listened to with the available tools, say so and ask me to check it. Never claim a review that wasn't done.

### How to use the @coach.agz clips
The current clips are simple edits. Treat them as the **baseline to upgrade from**, not the ceiling:
- **Keep:** the voice, the delivery, how the footage is framed, the topics and the brand look.
- **Upgrade:** everything about the edit. Apply this standard on top.

### Upgrade path: from simple to the target
Fill in the "Now" column after reviewing the actual clips. Until then it is a hypothesis.

| Area | Now (typical simple edit, to be confirmed) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Captions | Same style, same position, word-pop all the way through | Phone-safe layout. Brand type: Bebas for key lines, DM Sans for support. Accent words go gold or red as they're spoken. Strong lines hold still. Captions can sit behind the subject when it helps. |
| Graphics | Few or none, or a static card dropped on top | The carousel and overlay system comes to life: the kicker line draws on, tag pills land, stats count up, comparison rows tick off, the spectrum marker slides into place. Each one is tied to the spoken point. |
| Structure | Talking head with cuts | Talking head, then a full-screen graphic scene, then back to the talking head, joined by handoffs (the gold left line becomes a divider, an overlay card expands to full screen, a stat becomes the next title). |
| Camera | Static, or a constant punch-in | Steady while graphics build. Reframes and punch-ins only for emphasis or reveals. |
| Depth | Flat | Separate the subject from the background. Cards sit in front of or behind the person, with shadows and a soft glow. |
| Pace | One speed | Fast, dense builds for lists and data. Space for the hook, the key warning and the CTA. |
| Sound | Music bed only, or a whoosh on every cut | Hits on landings, soft ticks on rows, a low thud on big stats, quiet moments around warnings. Speech always clear. |
| Ending | Abrupt | The last scene resolves into a branded end card ("Screenshot this" schedule style). |
